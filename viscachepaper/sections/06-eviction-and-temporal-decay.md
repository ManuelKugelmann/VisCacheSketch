# 6. Eviction and Temporal Decay

Pressure-scaled eviction is always active on the insert path. Steps 0–2 are protected (no eviction at home slot or first two probes). From step 3, each step doubles the eviction threshold (8, 16, 32, 64, 128), enabling self-healing of long chains while giving fresh entries time to accumulate samples. Inline overflow decay uses a single InterlockedAdd to subtract 1/8 of both counters when total exceeds 0xE000 (~57K), keeping counts bounded so recent samples dominate. Integer shift-truncation preserves the mean ratio within ~0.003% at trigger counts. A known ABA race (read-then-subtract) can over-decay by ~1/waveSize ≈ 3% of decay events — negligible in practice.

For dynamic scenes, an optional background decay pass traverses 1/DECAY_PERIOD of the table per frame, subtracting 1/8 of both counters on each visit (factor 0.875 per pass). An entry not refreshed decays geometrically: after K passes, counts scale as (7/8)^K. To reach 1/1024 of the original count requires K ≈ 52 passes. At DECAY_PERIOD=60 (~1 s at 60 fps): full decay in ~52 s. At DECAY_PERIOD=15: full decay in ~13 s. A PI controller auto-tunes DECAY_PERIOD based on eviction/insert ratio (target ~10%), speeding decay under load and slowing it in static scenes. Active entries resist decay because their sample rate (hundreds of inserts/frame at L0) far exceeds the decay rate (one 1/8 subtraction per DECAY_PERIOD frames). Not needed for single-frame rendering.
