# 9. ReSTIR Integration

The visibility cache is algorithm-agnostic: it reduces shadow-ray cost for any method that evaluates pairwise point-to-point visibility — classical next-event estimation, instant radiosity, or photon mapping. ReSTIR [Bitterli et al. 2020; Ouyang et al. 2021] is not related work but a particularly good integration target — spatial reuse concentrates many pixels onto the same light or secondary hit, and a world-space cache amortizes their shared visibility queries automatically. We demonstrate three integration points, all using the same hash table.

## 9.1 Cache-Informed Light Selection

Replace V=1 in ReSTIR's target function p̂ with cached μ during initial candidate generation: p̂ = fs × Le × G × max(μ, μmin). The μmin floor (default 0.01) prevents permanent exclusion of visible lights with stale cache entries. Bokšanský and Meister [2025] independently apply the same visibility-weighted selection idea with a neural cache.

**Unbiasedness via defensive sampling.** The cached μ appears only in the target function p̂ used for candidate selection, not in the final estimator. ReSTIR's 1/W normalization cancels p̂ — the selected light's contribution is divided by its selection probability, which includes μ. For this cancellation to hold, every light must have nonzero selection probability — equivalently, the proposal must have full support (f̂ > 0 wherever f > 0). The μmin floor enforces this as *defensive sampling*: flooring v̂(x) ≥ ε guarantees support coverage, restoring unbiasedness at cost of small variance from the uniform mixture weight. Even a fully occluded light (true μ=0) retains at least ε = 1% of its BRDF-weighted selection weight. The parameter ε controls the trade-off: larger ε adds more uniform exploration (higher variance on well-predicted lights) but stronger support guarantees; smaller ε trusts the cache more aggressively.

An exploration candidate (1/M of budget, where M is the number of initial light candidates per pixel, typically 32) uses uniform sampling and always traces its shadow ray — the ε-greedy strategy. Combined with μmin, permanent exclusion is impossible.

L0 suffices for candidate weighting. Occluded lights (μ≈0) are effectively removed from the candidate pool, improving hit rate from ~70% to ~95% in scenes with many occluded lights.

## 9.2 Post-Shading Shadow Ray

After ReSTIR selects a light, apply prediction-with-correction (Algorithm 3) on the final shadow ray. Decoupled from ReSTIR internals — this integration point works identically whether the visibility query comes from ReSTIR DI, instant radiosity, or classical next-event estimation. Saves ~88% of final shadow rays. Modest but zero risk.

## 9.3 GI Revalidation

The cache's strongest integration case. Spatial reuse (ReSTIR GI [Ouyang et al. 2021] or ReSTIR PT [Lin et al. 2022] at any bounce depth) borrows neighbor paths and must verify visibility from the current shading point P to the neighbor's reconnection vertex Q. With k=5 spatial neighbors, unbiased revalidation costs 5 shadow rays per pixel — the main reason production systems use biased skip-revalidation.

Prediction-with-correction makes unbiased revalidation near-free: look up cached V(P, Q), apply contribution-weighted RR (Sec. 10). Expected traces drop from 5 to ~0.7 per pixel. This is where the cache's value is highest, because no screen-space alternative exists for arbitrary reconnection vertices — temporal reprojection and neighbor polling cannot help when Q is a world-space point unrelated to the current pixel's view.

ReSTIR PT generalizes ReSTIR GI [Lin et al. 2022, GRIS]: at maxSurfaceBounces=1 they produce the same estimator, but PT's hybrid shift (reconnection + random replay) handles specular first bounces where GI's reconnection-only shift degenerates to plain path tracing. We use ReSTIR PT for all GI integration tests.

| Post-shading | ~88% of 1/px | Trivially | Minimal |

## 9.4 World-Space Reservoirs (WS-ReSTIR DI)

Boissé [2021] keys ReSTIR DI reservoirs by a screen-space world-position hash; we follow the *grid-side* line of work (Boksanský et al. [2021], "ReGIR") and key directly by quantized primary-hit position `posA` at a configurable cell size. The cell hash is the same `pcgHashA` used by VisCache's A-side addressing — the two systems share the hash function as a building block but otherwise their cell domains are decoupled (VisCache uses a multi-level cascade with auto-tuned coarse/fine sizes; WS-ReSTIR uses a single user-configurable `wsCellSize` in world units).

**Cell key — normal axis.** Following Zhang et al. [2023] and Boissé [2021], we optionally fold a 6-axis face-normal bin into both the slot hash and the fingerprint salt (`gWSNormalAddr`). This prevents cross-orientation pollution at corners and thin shells where a single posA cell can straddle two surface orientations (e.g. a wall meeting a floor). The 6-axis bin (±X, ±Y, ±Z) is coarser than the continuous octahedral encoding used in some prior work but matches the granularity at which surface orientation actually changes for typical architectural / interior geometry; finer bins fragmented the cache without measurable quality gain in our tests. Off by default — the gain is concentrated at corner and grazing-angle pixels and trades cell capacity for orientation-correctness.

**Per-cell light pool (WSCellPool, ReGIR variant).** On top of the single-slot reservoir, each cell carries a small *pool* of N=8 light identities populated via on-the-fly RIS during the main pass and read back at the next pixel's K-RIS. Each pool entry stores `(lightTypeIndex, payload, sourcePdf)` so that drawing from the pool at a reading pixel is treated as a sample from the writer-side proposal pdf — RIS unbiases via `p̂(reader) / sourcePdf`, exactly as if the entry had been freshly drawn at the reader. Insertion is currently random-replace once full; the M-cap + decay schedule from Boksanský et al. [2021] is a one-line refinement we adopt next.

**Two-pass cell-pool fill.** A dedicated `PathTracerPrePass` instance with `wsCellPoolFillOnly=true` runs first; it does K-RIS candidate generation and pool inserts, but skips shading and the visibility ray. The main `PathTracer` instance then reads the populated pool. Both instances share the same `gWSCellPools` UAV; Falcor's render graph inserts the write→read barrier between them. This recovers RTXDI-style tile-pre-sampling semantics within a single frame, without the per-frame compute pass required by the original RTXDI design.

**Implementation status (2026-05-04, aligned-substrate parity build).** Ladder00 ships as the canonical baseline matrix with two reference comparators — `vanilla` (NEE single-bounce) and `rtxdi` (Falcor's RTXDIPass) — and two ours: `restir_2d` (pool addressed by 2D screen tile + per-pixel reservoir, RTXDI-faithful substrate) and `restir_3d` (pool addressed by 3D world cell at footprint-derived entry level + per-pixel reservoir, world-space analog). The two ours-variants now share a single shared core (`_run_baseline_restir` in `VisCache_LadderCommon.py`) and differ only in `wsPoolAddrMode` — every other knob (K=8 candidates, `mCap=5`, K_spatial=4 at radius=16 px, visibility-blind p̂, frame-accumulation SPP `actual_spp=1, num_frames=spp`) is identical. Cell pool fill is a dedicated `PathTracerPrePass` instance (`wsCellPoolFillOnly=true` + UAV barrier to the main pass), and `restir_3d` uses **footprint-derived entry level** via the shared `vhfLevelForFootprint(targetPx²)` helper (§4) so its cell projects to ~256 px² screen footprint at the primary hit, equating fill density with `restir_2d`'s 16-px tile.

**Pool = direct port of RTXDI's tile presample.** The cell pool is a literal port of RTXDI's tile-presample design [Wyman & Panteleev 2021] to the world-space cell hash. Each pool slot stores `(lightTypeIndex, payload, sourcePdf)` only — no per-writer pHat tracking, no Bitterli streaming-RIS at insert. `wsCellPoolInsert` is **random-pick a slot, last-writer-wins**: across many writers per cell per frame, slot contents converge to LightBVH's pdf distribution at the cell, exactly like RTXDI's tile slots converge to the pdf-mipmap distribution. Pool reads use **Convention B** (RTXDI's `targetPdf × invSourcePdf` math): the reader rebuilds the light sample at its shading point, computes `pHat_A = lum(weight × Li)` (Falcor's pdf-divided form) and `pdfReader = ls.pdf`, then injects into the local stream with `invPdf = pdfReader / sourcePdfWriter` — equivalent to `pHat_raw × (1/sourcePdfWriter)` since `pHat_A × pdfReader = pHat_raw`. The `pdfReader/sourcePdfWriter` ratio is the standard "writer ≠ reader proposal-domain correction" — for a fresh candidate drawn at the reader the ratio is 1 (matches K-RIS scale); for a pool candidate from a different writer's domain the ratio rebalances back into the reader's RIS stream.

**Why no Bitterli RIS at insert.** Earlier iterations attempted per-slot Bitterli streaming RIS with writer-pHat-weighted survivor selection and cross-pixel reservoir merge at read (`w = pHat_reader × wSum / pHat_writer × M_capped/M_slot`). These produced catastrophic regressions on emissive-heavy scenes (Sponza x4 from identity-hint baseline 8.37 to 22.42 with the broken double-pdf-divide convention bug; 12.17 after fixing the convention and the side-vs-area footprint param). The structural diagnosis: any per-slot pHat-weighted RIS at write-time bakes the writer's shading-point pHat into the slot's survivor selection. For cross-pixel cells (multiple writers with different pHats), the survivor is optimal for one writer but not the reader, biasing the merge math regardless of how clean the cross-pixel re-weight formula is. Standard Bitterli neighbor-merge assumes `pHat_writer ≈ pHat_reader` within a cell, which is violated when cell occupancy is heterogeneous (mixed BRDFs, mixed distances to light, mixed normal directions). RTXDI sidesteps this by storing only `sourcePdf` at tile slots (no pHat tracking, no writer-bias) and doing the RIS at the reader where pHat is unambiguously the reader's. We adopt the same choice.

**Substrate-equivalence claim.** `restir_2d` is RTXDI's own substrate (per-pixel reservoir + tile pool), so a numbers-match against `rtxdi` is "matches by construction" — it validates that our framework correctly implements that substrate, no novelty claim. `restir_3d` is the world-space analog of the same recipe (single-slot cell reservoir + 3D cell pool, both addressed by the shared cascade) — its parity against `restir_2d` validates the *substrate-equivalence subsumption*: with the same fill density and the same RIS-at-insert math, swapping screen-space tile addressing for world-space cell addressing reproduces tile-pool quality without screen reprojection (cell IDs are camera-invariant). The novelty is the curve beyond x1 single-frame: world-space reuse compounds across frames without motion-vector reprojection, and the cascade enables cross-resolution fill at no extra prepass cost.

**Gap to RTXDI on Sponza / BistroInterior / Cornell_3AL x1.** RTXDI's tile presample is shading-AGNOSTIC (a precomputed pdf-mipmap), so any tile slot is one pdf-sample of the unconditioned light distribution. Our cell pool fills via the main-pass + Bayer-sparse pre-pass using LightBVH's shading-CONDITIONAL proposal (LightBVH considers surface position when sampling). At read, we treat the pool entry as a Convention-A identity-hint candidate at the reader's pHat — the same way RTXDI treats its tile slot, just without the shading-agnostic guarantee. This adds a "writer ≠ reader pdf-domain" approximation: candidates were drawn under the writer's surface-conditional density; the reader's RIS resamples with its own pHat as if the writer = reader. The bias is bounded by pHat-variance within the cell (small for Cornell_1PL/32PL with deterministic point-light pHats; larger for Sponza/Bistro with mixed emissives at varied distances).

**Why we keep Convention A despite RTXDI's algorithm using Convention B.** RTXDI's `risWeight = targetPdf × invSourcePdf` is the proper unbiased RIS weight when proposal-pdfs are shading-AGNOSTIC (so `pdfReader == sourcePdfWriter` for any payload). We initially ported Convention B exactly — `w = pHat_A × (pdfReader / sourcePdfWriter)`, which equals `pHat_raw × invSourcePdfWriter` — but the `pdfReader/sourcePdfWriter` ratio explodes into fireflies on outdoor / env-map scenes (BistroExterior x4 reached 36–48 % mean error vs vanilla 18 %, max-err 100+ %). LightBVH's surface-conditional density makes the ratio variance unbounded; RTXDI's pdf-mipmap doesn't have this issue because the ratio is identically 1. Convention A drops the ratio (treats the slot as a fresh candidate at the reader) — the residual bias from `pHat_writer ≠ pHat_reader` is far smaller than Convention B's pdf-ratio variance for our use case. **A future port of RTXDI's pdf-mipmap as our pool-fill source would let us safely re-enable Convention B for the missing few-percent gain.**

**Earlier bugs and the diagnostic arc** (resolved 2026-05-04, full discussion deferred to Appendix):

1. *Dirac-pdf RIS weight = 0* — Falcor's `samplePointLight` returns `ls.pdf = 0`. Our original K-RIS used `w = pHat × (1/ls.pdf) = 0` for any Dirac light, silently disabling K-RIS for any scene with point or directional lights. The fix uses Convention-A throughout: `pHat = luminance(weight × ls.Li) = luminance(L_r)`, `invPdf = 1.0`. Works uniformly for area lights (`pdf > 0`) and Dirac (`pdf = 0`).
2. *Pool RIS-at-insert with writer-pHat-weighted survivor selection* — first attempt used Bitterli streaming RIS at write time (`w_i = pHat_writer × invPdf_i` per slot, replace with probability `w_i / wSum`). For cross-pixel cells this bakes the writer's shading-point pHat into the slot's survivor identity; readers see a survivor optimal for one writer but not for them. Sponza x4 went from identity-hint baseline 8.37 to merge-with-stored-pHat 12.17 to merge-with-broken-Conv-B 22.42. **Fix: drop Bitterli at insert entirely. Pool slot stores `(lightID, payload, sourcePdf)` only; insert is `random-pick a slot, last-writer-wins`** — RTXDI's tile-presample design literally. Across many writers per cell per frame, slot contents converge to the writers' joint pdf distribution.
3. *Convention B's pdf-ratio fireflies on env-map scenes* — initially read pool slots as `w = pHat_A × pdfReader/sourcePdfWriter` (RTXDI's exact `targetPdf × invSourcePdf` math); BistroExterior x4 reached 36–48 % mean error from variance amplification. **Fix: Convention A read** (`w = pHat_A × 1`) — see above.
4. *Footprint param semantic mismatch (3D cell ↔ 2D tile)* — `wsCellPoolFootprintPx=16` was interpreted as AREA inside `vhfLevelForFootprint` (→ 4-px-side cell), while `wsPoolTileSize=16` was SIDE LENGTH (→ 16-px tile). 16× area mismatch broke 2d↔3d substrate equivalence. **Fix: the cell-pool resolver squares its input before passing to `vhfLevelForFootprint`'s area-input**, so the same numeric parameter (16 = side length in pixels) matches between addressing modes.
5. *MIS skipped on pool/temporal/spatial reuse paths* — block 1a (the fresh K-RIS sample at NEE generation) applied `evalMIS(1, ls.pdf, 1, scatterPdf)` to dampen `ls.Li` by light-vs-BSDF MIS; blocks 1c (pool reads), 1.5 (temporal reuse), and 2.5 (spatial reuse) re-evaluated the light sample at the reader and computed `pHat = lum(weight × Li)` *without* re-applying MIS. Pool/reused candidates therefore had higher RIS weight than fresh by the MIS factor, biasing the resampled winner toward reused samples. **Fix: re-apply `evalMIS(1, ls.pdf, 1, scatterPdf)` at all three reuse sites, gated identically to block 1a (`kUseMIS && !applyRTXDI && lightType != Analytic`).** Sponza x1 dropped 1.26pp, BistroInt x1 dropped 0.96pp, BistroInt x4 dropped 0.86pp; BistroExterior x1 now beats RTXDI by 0.45–0.76pp.
6. *`kWSNormalCos` too strict for spatial reuse* — was 0.9 (~26° angular reject); RTXDI's `normalThreshold` default is 0.5 (~60°). Loosening to 0.5 increases neighbor diversity in spatial reuse without measurable bias on the test scenes, contributing further small improvements (BistroInt x4 −0.34pp on top of MIS fix).

**Visibility-aware fill (implemented as RIS-target multiplier).** The same pre-pass mechanism opens the door to the *V-aware re-presample* refinement that RTXDI explicitly declined as "prohibitively expensive": draw M power-weighted candidates per cell, weight each by `p̂ × V`, keep the top-N. Cell pool ends up with VISIBLE-bright candidates only — the bright-occluded sample bias on emissive-heavy scenes (Sponza/Bistro) is structurally eliminated. The cost RTXDI feared came from M shadow rays per fill; we sidestep that by reusing **VisCache's μ with the μmin floor** (§9.1) as the V signal at fill time. **A subtle math gotcha:** an early formulation of step (d) used probabilistic acceptance — insert with probability `max(μ, μmin)` and store `sourcePdf' = ls.pdf × accept`. This is *backwards* in the streaming-reservoir math: the rare 1% accept of an occluded candidate produces a 100× weight `pHat / (ls.pdf × 0.01)` at insert time, *amplifying* occluded entries' chance of capturing the slot via reservoir replacement. The corrected form (which we now implement) leaves `sourcePdf` unchanged and applies V as a *target-function multiplier*: insert weight is `(p̂ × max(μ, μmin)) / ls.pdf`. Slot replacement probability scales linearly with V; reader's `1/sourcePdf` RIS estimator uses the unmodified `ls.pdf` and remains unbiased. The pseudocode is now:

```
for each candidate at the cell:
    μ = vhfLookup(cellPosA → lightPosB)
    pHat_with_V = p̂ × max(μ, μmin)
    insert(sourcePdf = ls.pdf, ris_target = pHat_with_V)   # always-insert
    # Bitterli streaming reservoir per slot picks based on
    # weight = ris_target / sourcePdf = (p̂ × V) / ls.pdf
```

This is M cache reads instead of M rays — affordable even at full-screen prepass density, with the Bayer-sparsity from step (b) still available as a fill-scheduling lever rather than a cost necessity. Unbiasedness is preserved by the same defensive-sampling argument as §9.1: μmin floors the writer-side weighting, so a cold or stale cache cell cannot permanently exclude any light from the pool. The mechanism is identical to §9.1's cache-informed light selection — just shifted from *reader-side* (per-pixel candidate scoring) to *writer-side* (per-cell prepass fill), so the variance reduction lands in the pool's identities rather than per-pixel one-shot resampling.


**Unified addressing — one mechanism, three target footprints.** VisCache, WS-ReSTIR reservoirs, and the WS-ReSTIR cell pool all key on `(posA, faceN, level)` via the *same* primitives — there is one set of cascade helpers, used by all three:

- footprint-derived entry level: `lvl = cascadeLevelForFootprint(α × pixel_footprint)`
- jittered position quantization: `q = jitterQuantize(posA, vhfPosASize(lvl), salt(lvl))`
- normal-bin folded into both slot hash and fingerprint salt
- pcgHashA-derived `(slot, fingerprint)` address pair

The structures differ only in the *target footprint scale* used to pick the entry level — a single per-structure multiplier `targetFootprint = α × pixel_footprint`:

- **VisCache:** α ≈ 1 (≈1 cell per pixel) — finest cells; the cache wants high spatial resolution because visibility decisions are per-shading-point.
- **WS-ReSTIR reservoir:** α ≈ 8–16 — many pixels share one reservoir, so M grows fast across frames and temporal stability dominates.
- **WS-ReSTIR cell pool:** α ≈ 4 — a middle ground, bigger candidate union than VisCache without the temporal-stability bias of the reservoir.

**Relation to prior footprint-based work.** Binder et al. [2018, 2019] derive a *continuous* per-hit cell radius from the ray footprint at the shading point — `cellSize = α × footprint` — and store one entry at that size in a single hash grid. ReGIR [Boksanský et al. 2021] uses a fixed-resolution grid; Boissé [2022] adds a two-level promotion/decay policy on a fixed grid. Our contribution is the **simultaneous storage of entries at multiple sizes**: the cascade is N parallel grids, populated in parallel, each with its own log-spaced cell size. The discretization of footprint into integer level indices is incidental — it is the necessary precondition for the parallel-grid storage (you cannot key into "the grid at this continuous radius" because there is no finite address space for it). The substance is the parallel ensemble of grids and the prefill rule that writes to all grids whose cell size brackets the hit's footprint.

This buys three properties no single-resolution scheme provides:

1. **Shared addressing across data structures.** Different structures (VisCache, reservoir, pool) target different α values that land at different cascade levels; a single per-level table serves all of them, and a structure with α=1 can read entries written by a hit whose α=4 simply by reading the corresponding finer-level grid.
2. **Multi-level prefill.** "The parent cell" and "the finer cell containing this hit" are well-defined and there is exactly one of each. A hit prefills L−1, L, L+1 in three constant-time writes; reads at any of those three levels see the entry without further work.
3. **Cascade-descend on starvation.** A reader that needs more samples visits `level − 1`, `level − 2`, … and finds entries written by hits at finer footprints. Continuous-radius schemes have no comparable redundancy because no single radius brackets a *neighbourhood* of radii.

The trade is real — level-boundary discretization adds noise that Binder's continuous cell radius avoids — and we integrate it back out with stochastic LOD jitter (`gWSCellLevelJitter`) on top of three existing decay levers from the single-level VisCache, all of which are level-agnostic and apply unchanged to the cascade:

1. **Aging decay** — background table sweep, period auto-tuned by a PI controller on eviction load.
2. **Pressure-driven eviction** — cells that lose to a probe collision while underpopulated are reclaimed.
3. **Disagreement decay** (`accelDecayDisagreeThresh`) — an inserted sample that disagrees with the running mean by more than a threshold triggers an in-line half-decay of the cell before the add, giving outlier observations extra weight against a stale mean.

These three together cover the stale-entry cases we have observed: aging handles slow drift, pressure handles oversubscription, disagreement handles abrupt scene changes (a wall moves, a light switches off). No per-level decay policy is required in the current implementation, though dynamic per-level factors (e.g. faster decay at the level corresponding to a moving camera's currently-changing footprint) remain a natural addition if the parallel-grid storage starts to expose stale entries at off-target levels. The cascade is best read as Binder's footprint-derived cell size promoted from a single resolution per hit to a small ensemble of resolutions per hit, with the discretization as the price of admission.

The shared primitive is `cascadeLevelForFootprint(α × pixel_footprint)`; the rest (jitterQuantize, pcgHashA, normal-bin mixing, sourcePdf-weighted RIS) is invariant across the three. ReGIR and Boissé use fixed-resolution grids and so cannot share addressing across multiple data structures; we can.

**Bayer prepass density couples to pool cell footprint.** The fill-only prepass is Bayer-sparse: 1 of `N_bayer²` pixels is active per frame. To deliver `M` fill samples per pool cell, the cell footprint must be `M × N_bayer²` pixels in area — equivalently, cell side ≈ `√M × N_bayer` pixels. The two parameters trade off directly:

- BayerN=1 (full prepass): cell side `√M` pixels — fine cells, full-screen prepass cost.
- BayerN=4 (4×4 sparse): cell side `4√M` pixels — coarser cells, prepass cost ≈0.06× vanilla.
- BayerN=8 (8×8): cell side `8√M` pixels — coarsest cells we'd typically use, prepass cost ≈0.016× vanilla.

For the natural target of `M = pool_N = 8` (each cell fills exactly once per Bayer cycle), this fixes the level selector's α from the chosen BayerN: pick the cost budget first, then the cell size follows. Multi-level prefill compounds this: each cascade level can run on its own Bayer schedule sized to its α, so the coarse-end fills cheaply across many levels in a single prepass dispatch.

**Hash-address axes beyond `(posA, faceN)`.** VisCache adds a `posB` axis to its key for pairwise-receiver visibility queries. The pool and reservoir use only `(posA, faceN)` today — sufficient for direct-illumination NEE on diffuse surfaces, where the proposal is view-independent. Useful extensions for GI / specular use cases: a `viewDir` axis on the reservoir (per-orientation reservoirs handle highlight transitions); a *light-direction* bin on the pool (partitions entries by direction from cell to light, so a reader querying a specific bounce direction gets direction-conditioned candidates); a *distance-to-light* bin (separates near-vs-far emitters mixed in one cell). None are needed for our current pure-DI integration; the hash family is designed to admit them as additional salts without restructuring the addressing helpers.

**Cell-pool spatial reuse via jittered lookup.** The single-slot reservoir has a jittered-neighbour gather loop (`wsResolveJitteredCell`) that perturbs `posA` in the surface tangent plane and re-runs the same hash. The pool gets the same treatment: a reader queries home + N jittered neighbour pools, drawing K_home candidates from home and K_nbr from each neighbour. With K_home=8 + 3 neighbours × K_nbr=4, the effective pool size jumps to 20 candidates — closer to RTXDI's M=32 initial sample count without needing coarser cells. Each entry already carries its own `sourcePdf` (writer's NEE proposal pdf), so the union is unbiased: the reader's RIS resamples by `1/sourcePdf` regardless of which pool the entry came from. The same defensive-sampling argument as §9.1 applies — every reachable entry has nonzero selection probability.

**Multi-level prefill on overlapping cells.** A hit whose footprint resolves to entry level L writes its candidate not just to L, but also to L−1 (the unique parent cell containing the L cell) and L+1 (the unique finer-level cell containing the hit point). Three writes per insert, ~3× prepass write throughput, ~0.2× vanilla per frame at 1/16 Bayer-sparsity. The payoff: the cascade is never empty within ±1 level of the populated entry level. A reader that coarsens from L due to starvation finds writes from hits whose target was L (in the parent at L−1); a reader that refines via stochastic LOD jitter or smaller footprint finds writes from those same hits (in the relevant L+1 child). Because each pool entry carries its own `sourcePdf`, reads from L−1, L, or L+1 are unbiased — the reader's RIS resamples by `1/sourcePdf` regardless of which level the entry was inserted at, so the cascade level only changes spatial catchment, not estimator correctness.


**Active level range is hit-driven.** While the LOD-level *scaling* (level 0 → coarsest cell, level N-1 → finest) is set once per scene from world-space bounds (auto-tuned via `posACoarse` and a per-level step factor), the *active subset* of levels in any given frame is determined by where the camera's rays land. Close-up surfaces sit at fine levels, distant ones at coarse — a Sponza viewpoint typically activates 3–5 contiguous levels out of 8. Two optimizations follow that the prior footprint-only and fixed-grid schemes cannot make:

1. **Pre-pass effort proportional to the active histogram.** A short query phase at frame start measures the per-level hit distribution (one-line atomic increment per primary hit). The Bayer-sparse pre-pass then targets fill effort at populated levels and skips the empty ones — pool capacity not wasted on levels nothing reads from.
2. **Inactive-level pool reuse.** Levels with zero hits in the past K frames release their pool slots back to a free list, recycled by levels currently under pressure. Total pool memory stays bounded at ~one frame's worth of *active* levels regardless of cascade depth, decoupling the storage budget from `numLevels`.

**Boundary smoothing across levels — jittered footprint vs. blended read.** A pixel whose footprint sits near a level boundary can either (a) read both adjacent levels and blend by footprint-distance to each level edge, or (b) jitter the footprint stochastically and pick the resulting single level deterministically. Option (b) — the same trick used for stochastic mipmap LOD selection — keeps one pool read per pixel and no extra arithmetic, trading boundary-blend for boundary-noise that integrates out across pixels and frames. It composes with our existing spatial jitter machinery (`gWSJitterFilter`, `gWSJitterCell`): adding `gWSJitterLevel` gives the same boundary-smoothing along the level axis with one extra uniform random per pixel and no new code path.

**Reservoir, pool, and VisCache: three structures, one trust calculus, three commitment postures.** All three share the same accounting — sample count gated by variance to express trust — but they differ in *what gets committed* and therefore in how they can spread across cells.

| Structure | Commits to | Spread-out reuse | Revalidation needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reservoir | one chosen sample + `W` estimator | k-neighbour merge | yes — borrowed sample's visibility from new shading point |
| Cell pool | bag of N candidate identities (un-resampled) | union of home + neighbour cells | no — p̂ re-evaluated at reader |
| VisCache | μ histogram per cell-pair | already shared freely | N/A — visibility *is* the value |

The pool and VisCache are more alike than reservoir-vs-pool: both are observational histograms with no commitment to a single output, both tolerate spatial spread without revalidation. The reservoir is the outlier — its commitment to a winner is what forces revalidation when borrowed, and what makes our paper's prediction-with-correction (§9.3) so useful. Three direct consequences:

1. **The three structures need not share a cell.** Reservoir, pool, and VisCache can each sit at its own cascade level and key. Reservoir wants coarse (shared M grows fast → temporal stability); pool is tunable (coarser = bigger candidate union, finer = sharper importance prior); VisCache is per-pair and decoupled by construction.
2. **Pool reads can union neighbour cells.** The "effective pool" is `concat(home, jittered_1, jittered_2, ...)`; each entry carries its own `sourcePdf` and the reader RIS-resamples, so the union is unbiased without further bookkeeping. This separates spatial averaging width from cell key size — wider catchment without coarser keys.
3. **Pool spreading is strictly cheaper than reservoir spreading.** The reservoir-share path costs one shadow ray per borrowed reservoir for revalidation (or one PWC lookup with our cache); the pool-share path costs only the K-RIS evaluations the reader was going to do anyway.

**Cascade entry and traversal — different rules for different structures.** The pool and the reservoir do not carry a natural per-cell variance signal; they hold sample bags / RIS state, not Bernoulli observations. So their cascade traversal has only one direction:

- **Pool / reservoir:** enter at the footprint-derived level (target ≈ 1 cell per pixel footprint). If `count` (pool) or `M` (reservoir) is below the starvation threshold, *ascend* (coarsen) one level, repeat. A max-footprint cap prevents runaway coarsening into spatially-meaningless cells. Variance plays no role.

- **VisCache:** the cell *does* carry a Bernoulli variance estimate (μ and σ² over μ at every cell-pair). VisCache traversal is bidirectional: coarsen on starvation as above, *and* refine on inconsistency — a cell with enough samples but high σ² is straddling a visibility discontinuity, and the finer level's better spatial discrimination earns its place. The variance gate is already used elsewhere in the cache (`vt`, `stderrThreshold`); the cascade simply consumes its output.

Net design parameters: pool/reservoir get one knob (`count_min` / `M_floor`) plus the footprint cap (`footprintMaxScale`); VisCache uses its existing variance gate plus the same starvation knob. The pool's simpler rule reflects its simpler role — it is a candidate bag, not a posterior over a quantity, and refining on a non-existent variance signal would be a category error.

## 9.5 Per-Cell Partial Path with Generator (proposed)

Sections 9.1–9.4 lift visibility (cached `μ`) and light-sample candidates (`WSCellPool`) from per-pixel structures into world-space cells riding the same `posA` cascade. The natural successor lifts the *path* into the cell — specifically, the partial path produced by a ReSTIR PT initial sample and the random seed that generated it. We sketch the architecture and three theoretical claims; implementation is left to a follow-up once the WSCellPool slot type is generalized.

**MK2006 §3 sketched this design 17 years ago in a different framing:** "store partial paths in cells together with their generating path." That prefigures the GRIS reservoir. The ReSTIR PT (Lin 2022) reservoir's `(rcVertex, initRandomSeed)` *is* a partial-path with its generator: the seed materializes the prefix path lazily via random replay, the rcVertex is the postponed-reconnection point. Hybrid-shift MIS + Jacobian (Lin 2022) is the unbiasedness piece MK2006 lacked. ReSTIR PT does this *per pixel*; ReGIR / Boissé 2021 / our `WSCellPool` does it *per cell* but only for final shadow connections; Zhang 2023 caches whole sub-paths *per cell* but without GRIS hybrid-shift integration. The empty cell of the substrate-convergence table is per-cell partial-path-with-generator + hybrid shift — proposed here.

**Substrate-convergence table — the missing diagonal:**

| Storage / commitment | Final shadow only | rcVertex + generator | Full sub-paths |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-pixel reservoir | ReSTIR DI [Bitterli 2020] | **ReSTIR PT [Lin 2022]** | — |
| Per-cell reservoir | ReGIR [Boksanský 2021], WS-ReSTIR DI [Boissé 2021], WSCellPool (§9.4) | **§9.5 proposed** | Zhang 2023 |

**Architecture: hashmap-in-cell, seed-keyed open addressing.** Each cell carries `K=8` slots laid out as an open-addressed hashmap keyed by the slot's `initRandomSeed`. Slot payload extends the WSCellPool layout:

```
struct WSPathCellSlot {
    uint   initRandomSeed;          // EMPTY sentinel = 0xFFFFFFFF
    uint   rcVertexInstID;
    uint   rcVertexBary_2x16unorm;  // following Lin 2026 §8 compression
    uint   rcVertexWi_oct2x16;      // octahedral, 2x16unorm
    float3 rcVertexRadiance;
    float  W;                       // unbiased contribution weight (GRIS UCW)
    uint   M;                       // structural dedup count — see Claim 1
};
```

Insert is lock-free CAS-loop over `K` slots with linear probing starting at `initRandomSeed & (K−1)` (the seed is already a high-entropy PCG output, so modulo-`K` is uniform): a seed match bumps `M` and merges weight, an empty slot is CAS-claimed, and a full table of distinct seeds falls back to weighted reservoir sampling for unbiased replacement. Cell-address layer is unchanged from §9.4 — same `pcgHashA(posA, faceN)` over the existing cascade, same checksum-disambiguated open-addressed top-level hash. Total cost: ~64 B/slot × 8 = 512 B/cell, on the same order as our existing WSCellPool (72 B × 8). Cells decay via the existing temporal mechanism (§6); slots with `M` falling below threshold rejoin the free pool for reuse, so capacity is governed by the active level set rather than the worst-case theoretical cascade depth.

**Why open-addressed hashmap inside the cell rather than Zhang 2023's indirection layout.** Zhang [2023] keeps reservoirs in screen-space per-pixel buffers and stores only *indices* into them per cell, with checksum-disambiguated identity and variable per-cell occupancy bounded by a global `SampleIndexBuffer`. That layout supports unbounded sample counts per cell, but cannot dedup samples by seed at the cell level — the same partial path computed in two pixels lives in two reservoirs that the cell merely lists. Recovering Lin 2026 §5's seed-based correlation gate would require a separate post-pass over the SampleIndexBuffer. Our hashmap-in-cell collapses that pass into a structural property of the cell layout: at most one slot per `initRandomSeed`, and the slot's `M` is the duplication count by construction (Claim 1 below).

**Claim 1 — Decorrelation upgrade: subsumes Lin 2026 §5 duplication maps structurally.** Lin et al. [2026] reduce ReSTIR-PT correlation artifacts by a per-frame compute pass that, for each pixel, counts how many of the 17×17 surrounding pixels share the same `initRandomSeed` (288 = `(17² − 1) / 1`); the resulting *duplication score* `D ∈ [0, 1]` adaptively reduces the temporal confidence cap via `c_Cap = lerp(c_default, c_min, D^α)` with `α = 0.1`. The pass costs ~1 ms/frame at 1080p and trades ~3% absolute relative bias inside correlated regions for the elimination of correlation blobs.

In §9.5's hashmap-in-cell, the same statistic is read directly off `slot.M` for free: since insertion deduplicates by seed, `slot.M` is exactly "how many pixels' partial paths share this `initRandomSeed` at this rcVertex cell." The corresponding cell-level confidence cap is `c_Cap_cell = lerp(c_default, c_min, (M / K_pixels_in_cell)^α)`, with no compute pass needed. Strictly stronger than Lin 2026's screen-space window: cells span more than 17 pixels at higher cascade levels, so cell-level decorrelation catches correlations that the 17×17 window misses by definition. The cost is the same `K · sizeof(slot)` we would have paid for a per-cell reservoir bag without seed dedup — Claim 1 reframes a previously paid storage cost as a free decorrelation primitive.

**Claim 2 — Cold-cache fix: WS-ReSTIR-DI lock-in vanishes when the cell stores the partial path instead of resolved samples.** Our current `WSCellPool` (§9.4) stores resolved light samples with visibility `V` baked into the survivor selection. At cold cache (frame 0, x1 SPP, RTXDI-blind p̂), K-RIS without `V` in p̂ produces a 2× worse error than vanilla on Bistro/Sponza — an issue we documented (`project_wsrestir_visibility_blind_bias.md`) and worked around by gating the pool fill on cached `μ` with the §9.1 defensive-sampling floor.

§9.5's slot stores only the partial path *and* its `initRandomSeed`. Visibility is *not* baked in: at retrieval, the reader's K-RIS performs random-replay from `initRandomSeed` to materialize the prefix, applies the hybrid-shift Jacobian to reach its own primary hit, and re-evaluates a fresh shadow ray from its own shading point to the cell-stored `rcVertex`. This is precisely Kugelmann [2006, §3.4]'s control-variate-with-Russian-roulette principle — *cache the variance-cheap geometric quantity, recompute the variance-expensive boolean (visibility) on demand* — applied 17 years later with the GRIS unbiasedness machinery the original sketch lacked. The §9.5 cell has no notion of cold-cache lock-in: a stale `(rcVertex, seed)` produces a fresh shadow ray every time it is read, and the §10 contribution-weighted Russian roulette still gates the cost.

**Claim 3 — Footprint-criteria alignment: Lin 2026 §4 reconnection thresholds become a cascade property by construction.** Lin et al. [2026, §4] derive a primary-ray footprint `R_pri² = ‖x₀ − x₁‖² · ⟨n_{x₁}, x₁→x₀⟩ / (4π)` and require any candidate reconnection vertex `x_k` on a path `x̄` to satisfy a *dual* footprint test: both `(p_{k−1}(ω_{k−1}) · G(x_{k−1} → x_k))^{−1}` and `(p_k(ω_k) · G(x_k → x_{k−1}))^{−1}` must exceed `c · R_pri²` with `c = 0.02`. This replaces ReSTIR PT 2022's scene-tuned distance-and-roughness pair (our `nearFieldDistance × sceneRadius` plus `specularRoughnessThreshold`) with a single scene-independent constant.

In §9.5, the cell's level in our cascade gives a footprint by construction: `posA_level_size² ∝ R_pri²` along the dominant axis at the level the writer used. The reconnection-validity test "is the reader's footprint contained in the writer's level envelope?" reduces to a level comparison — already what `wsLevelOffset` does in §9.4 for visibility cells. Lin 2026's per-pixel footprint formalism and our cascade level selection are two faces of the same primitive: identical closed-form `R_pri`, used in [Lin 2026] for shift-validity on a path domain and in our cache for cell-size on a hash domain. §9.5 unifies them — a writer's cascade level *is* its acceptance footprint, and a reader at a level whose footprint envelope contains the writer's is by construction a valid reconnection target. No `c` knob to tune; no per-scene `nearFieldDistance`; no roughness threshold (Lin 2026 §4's footprint test already subsumes it for parametric materials, and §11 of their supplemental gives the PDF-based fallback for black-box BSDFs).

**Why §9.5 is testable on our existing cascade.** None of these three claims requires new addressing. The §9.4 WSCellPool is a single slot-type generalization (light sample → partial-path-with-generator) away from the §9.5 layout — the cascade, the cell address hash, the temporal decay, the variance-gated level traversal (§9.4 closing paragraph), and the integration points (§9.1 cache-informed selection, §9.2 post-shading PWC, §9.3 GI revalidation) all carry over unchanged. Validation plan: a Ladder15+ step that runs `WSPathCell` in place of `WSCellPool` at the §9.4 build's exact knob settings, measured against (a) RTXDIPass + ReSTIR PT baseline (our `restir_3d` reference) and (b) Lin 2022 ReSTIR PT with screen-space spatial reuse only. The three claims yield three quantitative predictions: (1) correlation-blob count drops without a 17×17 compute pass (Lin 2026 §5 ablation re-run on cell-level confidence caps), (2) Sponza/Bistro x1-SPP error closes to vanilla without §9.1 cache gating (the cold-cache lock-in regression vanishes when no `V` is baked into the stored sample), and (3) `nearFieldDistance` and `specularRoughnessThreshold` can be removed from the parameter set without per-scene quality regression.
